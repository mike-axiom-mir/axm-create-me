The line-control representation is technically readable, but many Weather streaks collapse toward very thin hairlines. In the `path_eye` camera especially, they can read more like incidental renderer/debug traces than a deliberate atmosphere layer. The source-authored width variation is therefore visually under-expressed even though the broad world remains readable.

### Source-width candidate

The source-width ribbon candidate makes the same Weather field slightly more intentional and coherent without changing its sparse character. Individual streaks remain secondary to the pavilion, path and vegetation; the candidate does not form a broad veil or pull the eye away from primary world silhouettes.

The stronger width also helps the Weather field read as one designed environmental layer rather than isolated hairlines. The effect is still restrained enough that the current scene hierarchy remains intact.

The five explicitly retained near-plane clips are not visually persuasive as a reason to reject the candidate in these captured views: no inspected frame shows a distracting spike, wedge or abrupt dominant ribbon.

### Limitation visible in both versions

Neither version is a final atmosphere look. The proof remains deliberately simple and graphic: hard line/ribbon forms, no claim of final softness, volumetric depth, physically based precipitation, final color grading or cinematic atmosphere. Preferring the candidate therefore means **prefer this source-width presentation over the renderer-default thin-line control in this exact proof**, not that the atmosphere system is finished.
