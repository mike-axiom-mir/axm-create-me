### Constant `0.66`

The constant control preserves a clean hierarchy but reads comparatively flat on the broad `service_dark` panels. This is a visual observation only; it is not a claim that constant roughness is physically wrong.

### Bounded `0.60–0.72`

The bounded candidate adds visible surface breakup without changing silhouette, albedo hierarchy, component ownership, material role, or the established service-surface scale. The response is appropriately restrained in the mid-open views and becomes more legible when the opened inner lid catches the stronger peak-open light. That variation helps the broad service surfaces read as manufactured material rather than a uniformly flat proof surface.

The current periodic field is still diagnostic, not final texture art. Its usefulness here is the **response envelope**: enough roughness variation to break flatness, not enough to make the procedural pattern become the visual subject.

Measured candidate-vs-constant changed pixels above the established one-LSB gate:

- `mid_open / three_quarter`: `7,284 / 508,400 = 1.432730%`, max RGB-channel delta `0.019608`;
- `mid_open / front_interior`: `8,779 / 508,400 = 1.726790%`, max `0.019608`;
- `peak_open / three_quarter`: `41,891 / 508,400 = 8.239772%`, max `0.062745`;
- `peak_open / front_interior`: `54,428 / 508,400 = 10.705744%`, max `0.066667`;
- total changed pixels above one LSB: `112,382`.

These values demonstrate renderer-visible response; they are not aesthetic thresholds by themselves.

### Over-contrast `0.38–0.94`

The deliberate negative is visually too strong for the current `service_dark` family. The repeated light/dark bands become pattern-led, especially across the opened inner lid, and compete with the manufactured form/material hierarchy rather than merely breaking surface flatness.

The negative produces `203,968` changed pixels above one LSB across the same four contexts, with maximum RGB-channel deltas reaching `0.407843`. Again, the rejection is based on direct visual behavior, not on a universal numeric cutoff.
